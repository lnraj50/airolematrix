How to choose your stack

Start with one general assistant, one role-specific tool, and your existing workplace applications. Add another tool only when it solves a distinct recurring problem.

For developers, evaluate Cursor, Codex, Claude Code, or GitHub Copilot as starting options—not a requirement to buy all four. For business analysts, evaluate Miro AI, Lucid AI, or Whimsical AI for process diagrams and add an approved assistant for drafting requirements.

UI, UX and journey practice notes

Different scopes: UI focuses on interface presentation; UX covers usability and the experience of completing user goals; customer journey work connects touchpoints over time and across channels; service blueprinting adds the people, processes and systems behind that experience. Specialist responsibilities often overlap across organizations.

AI can accelerate drafting, prototyping and synthesis, but does not replace user research, accessibility testing, customer evidence or operational validation. Synthetic personas and generated emotions are hypotheses—not research findings. Keep current-state maps separate from proposed future-state experiences.

Figma capabilities vary by product, plan, seat, file permission and rollout. Miro AI mapping and synthesis require suitable feature access and approved data. Listed stacks are practical recommendations, not confirmation of enabled access or automatic integration.

How to combine frameworks without creating complexity

Sales: choose SPIN or Sandler to guide discovery, and BANT or MEDDPICC according to buying complexity. Use Challenger only when you have a defensible commercial insight. Do not mandate every model on every interaction.

Marketing: use STP to select audiences, 7Ps to align the offer, SOSTAC to structure the plan, and RACE to organize lifecycle execution. Apply PESO to media integration, AIDA to individual creative assets, and ICE to a transparent test backlog. AARRR and Lean Analytics are useful where product activation and cohort behavior can be measured.

Service: use KCS for trustworthy reusable knowledge, ISO 10002 guidance for complaints, SERVQUAL dimensions to diagnose perceived quality, and PDCA or DMAIC for improvement. Use ITIL where digital service management needs structured governance.

Metrics are not frameworks: satisfaction, effort, recommendation, first-contact resolution, resolution time, retention, acquisition cost and lifetime value are measurements that can support these approaches. Define the question, denominator, time window and collection method before comparing results. This update adds no survey scores or claimed business results.

Already covered: customer journey mapping and service blueprinting remain in the existing specialist section; this update adds operating and decision disciplines around them rather than counting them again as new frameworks.

Adoption and safety checklist

  1. Pilot one recurring task. Compare the AI-assisted result with your current manual result for accuracy, review effort and usefulness.
  2. Define a review gate. Code needs tests; analysis needs reconciliation; diagrams need process-owner sign-off; research needs primary-source checks.
  3. Use approved data only. Redact confidential, personal, customer, financial and credential information unless the tool and account are approved for it.
  4. Check access before purchasing. Power BI Copilot has capacity/licensing and tenant requirements; other Copilots and specialist tools have their own eligibility and usage limits.
  5. Get recording consent. Meeting assistants require appropriate consent, access controls and retention policies.
  6. Put agents behind controls. Use least-privilege integrations, test cases, logs, exception handling and human approval for consequential actions.
  7. Review high-stakes outputs. AI is not a substitute for qualified legal, medical, financial, security or employment decisions.

Best principle: choose by workflow fit, data governance, existing environment and verification burden—not by the longest feature list.
